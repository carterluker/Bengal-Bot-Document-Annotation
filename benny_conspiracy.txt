THE BENNY FILES: WHAT IDAHO STATE DOESN'T WANT YOU TO KNOW ABOUT THE BENGAL

An Investigative Essay (Please Do Not Fact-Check It)

Every great conspiracy begins with a simple question. Mine was: why has nobody ever seen Benny the Bengal eat?

Think about it. Benny has attended hundreds of football games, basketball games, and at least one extremely sweaty pancake breakfast. Not once, in any photograph, has he been caught holding a hot dog, a churro, or even a sad Holt Arena nacho. Benny is a large tiger. Large tigers eat. This is, as they say in the investigative community, "a red flag the size of a tiger."

I have since spent several nights staring at a corkboard and a ball of yarn, and I am ready to share my findings.

---

PART ONE: THE FUR DOES NOT SHED

Real tigers shed. Anyone who has owned a cat knows that a single feline can coat an entire apartment, three sweaters, and one emotionally significant casserole in fur. Yet Benny, who is allegedly a tiger of tremendous size, has never left so much as a whisker on the Pocatello city limits.

Where does the fur go? Into the walls of the Stephens Performing Arts Center, according to a source I made up. The university has a vested interest in keeping the shedding quiet. A tiger who sheds is a tiger who has to be vacuumed. A tiger who has to be vacuumed is a tiger on a payroll. And a tiger on a payroll raises uncomfortable questions about the Benny line item in the athletics budget, which I have never looked at, and do not intend to.

---

PART TWO: THE SUSPICIOUS LACK OF BIRTH CERTIFICATE

Idaho State University was founded in 1901. Benny the Bengal, by contrast, has no documented origin. There is no birth record, no adoption papers, and no sign that a baby Benny ever existed at all. He simply appeared, fully grown, wearing an orange-and-black jersey, like a mascot version of Athena springing from the head of Zeus, if Zeus were a Division I athletic department.

Some will say, "Carter, mascots are created by costume designers." To those people I say: that is exactly what they want you to say.

---

PART THREE: THE ALARMING ELEVATION OF POCATELLO

Pocatello sits at roughly 4,400 feet above sea level. Bengal tigers are native to the warm lowlands of the Indian subcontinent. They do not belong in the high desert of southeastern Idaho, where winter regularly dips below zero and the wind has opinions.

How, then, does Benny survive? Either (a) he is not a Bengal tiger, or (b) he has access to technology far beyond what the university has publicly acknowledged. I believe the answer is (b). I believe there is a heated underground tiger bunker beneath campus, and I believe the entrance is somewhere near the Rendezvous Complex, behind a vending machine that only accepts Bengal Bucks.

---

PART FOUR: THE GAME-DAY ANOMALY

Have you ever noticed that Benny is always at the game, but you never, ever see him arrive? There is no moment where he walks through a parking lot. There is no moment where he is seen waiting in line at a gas station. He is just... there. Dancing. Pointing at the crowd. Doing a bit with a giant foam finger.

Is Benny teleporting? Is there a tunnel network? Is Benny actually three or four identical tigers working in shifts, like the world's most committed rotating security detail? The answer, my sources suggest, is "probably the tunnel network," and my sources are two raccoons I spoke to behind the dining hall.

---

PART FIVE: THE THING ABOUT THE HANDS

Benny has hands. Tigers have paws. Benny can high-five, wave, hold a megaphone, and, on one occasion I am convinced happened, shake a stranger's hand with the firm confidence of a regional sales manager.

This is not tiger behavior. This is not even good tiger behavior. This is the behavior of an entity that has studied tigers from a distance and is doing its best.

I am not saying Benny is a human in a suit. I am saying Benny is a human in a suit that has convinced an entire university to pretend he is not a human in a suit. That is a much more impressive feat, and frankly, one we should respect.

---

PART SIX: THE FOLLOW-THE-MONEY SECTION

Every conspiracy needs a follow-the-money section, so here it is: Benny sells T-shirts. Benny sells foam fingers. Benny sells small plush Bennys, which are, I would like to point out, tiny tigers that people willingly take into their homes. Who benefits? The university. Who else benefits? Possibly Big Tiger Fur, a lobby I invented about four minutes ago but am now fully committed to.

---

CONCLUSION: WHAT DO WE DO NOW?

I do not know. Honestly, I think we do nothing. Benny is a wonderful mascot. He rallies the crowd, he poses for photos with toddlers, and he has never once been caught embezzling. If Benny is hiding a vast underground tiger empire, then at least it is a spirited one.

But I will leave you with this: the next time you see Benny at a game, look him in the eyes, and ask him one simple question.

"Benny... where do you keep the snacks?"

If he hesitates, even for a second, you will know.

GO BENGALS.

(This essay is a work of satire. Benny the Bengal is, to the best of anyone's knowledge, a delightful mascot and not the head of a subterranean tiger syndicate. Probably.)
